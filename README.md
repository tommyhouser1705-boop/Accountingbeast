# Ledger Lane
Two local browser accounting simulations. Requires Node.js 22 or newer, no dependencies or credentials. Run `npm start` in this directory for the server on port 3000; `npm test` runs the accounting-engine tests. Standalone HTML builds inline the stylesheet and scripts in index.html order.

## Introduction to Accounting
Students choose a business and complete a guided tour. The simulation follows January–December 2026. Purchases and generated monthly activity create dated source records, independent of student journal entries. Posting can be deferred through the records inbox. The general ledger uses T accounts. Students reconcile Cash against an independent bank statement, prepare monthly statements, and correct audit findings. Equipment payments clear the bank the following month; other cash transactions clear immediately.

Rent can cover one, three or six months. Multi-month payments create Prepaid Rent, followed by monthly expense adjustments. The year-end goal requires equipment depreciation (five-year useful life, zero salvage value, full months from purchase), annual financial statements, closing revenue and expenses through Income Summary into Owner Capital, and a final audit. Closing entries are excluded from the displayed annual statements. Grading uses original journal accuracy (80%) and first-attempt statements (20%); profit and market luck are not graded. Audits and reconciliation are required completion checks.

## Managerial Accounting
Separate game, saved progress and journal. Students plan a product business with prices, capacity, standard direct production costs, fixed factory costs and advertising. They compute contribution margin, break-even, budgeted profit, flexible-budget profit and the profit variance. Cost journals cover raw materials, direct labor payable, work in process, applied overhead, finished goods, cost of goods sold and under/overapplied overhead. Routine materials purchases, cash sales and advertising are automatically posted and ungraded. Months 1–3 use actual direct materials; month 4 onward introduces standard materials costing and unfavorable materials price variances where actual costs exceed the standard. Direct materials are 60% and direct labor 40% of the standard variable production cost. All units produced are completed and sold in the month; this is a simplified manufacturing scenario. A twelve-month portfolio records first-attempt calculation and cost-journal accuracy. Suggested grade: 50% journal entries, 50% calculations.

## Limits
Local browser storage only, no accounts, backend, live leaderboard or authenticated grading. Exported portfolios are student-controlled, not tamper-proof. Introductory business types share simplified service economics. Taxes, formal bank feeds, deposit-in-transit scenarios, full manufacturing variance analysis and instructor configuration are not implemented. Classroom fines and replacement corrections are teaching mechanics, not real-world audit procedures. Separate course-specific saves are retained when switching games. Full-screen and tour controls are available in the browser.

## Current active version: Introductory financial accounting only
Managerial scripts are retained for future work but are not loaded in index.html and no course selection appears. The active source-document view replaces journal prompts with invoices, receipts, deposit slips, remittances, sales summaries, usage reports, rental schedules and year-end asset schedules. Selecting a record opens its document in a dialog; students can open the journal form next to the document or defer it. Tour steps use a single native scrollable dialog with back/next/skip and no page highlights. The current standalone release is Ledger-Lane-v7.html.

## Business packages and financing
Business-specific supplies, equipment levels, fixed-price advertising and three locations replace arbitrary dollar inputs. Locations change demand; equipment limits service revenue capacity; supplied materials determine the service cost rate. Package screens explain the effects and show cash before/after. There is no required cash reserve: students may spend all available funds. Purchases cannot overdraw Cash. Additional owner or outside-investor contributions ($1,500/$3,000/$5,000) are available through Raise capital, including from a purchase screen. Investor contributions post to Investor Capital and financing cash flows, not revenue. Statements show combined contributed capital. Ownership percentages and investor profit-sharing are not simulated. Legacy oversized rents can be replaced by selecting an affordable location; earlier payments are preserved.

## Current schedule: four quarterly rounds
Quarter 1 January–March, Quarter 2 April–June, Quarter 3 July–September, Quarter 4 October–December. Internal reporting period IDs are 1/4/7/12 for compatibility with the annual workflow; source dates use the actual quarter calendar. Students type transaction dates on journal entries; missing or mismatched dates prevent posting. An explicit checklist routes students through unposted records, bank reconciliation, and financial statements, then exposes Start Quarter N both in financial submission feedback and on the business page. Monthly saves are backed up and can be downloaded before starting a quarterly game.

Quarter 2 introduces a supply invoice on account; Quarter 3 pays it and receives a customer advance; Quarter 4 earns the advance and accrues unpaid wages. Rent/prepaid coverage and revenue are aggregated for each quarter. Customer mix and collections vary. Quarter-end rent payments clear the bank next quarter; other cash movements clear within the quarter. Equipment depreciation uses actual acquisition months, including October acquisitions, not internal period IDs. Seasonal storefront scenes and equipment visuals change between quarters. Monthly managerial scripts are inactive. This prototype is not published or an authenticated classroom grading platform.

## Current active schedule: September–December
The business opens September 1, 2026. Four monthly rounds (period IDs 9/10/11/12) replace the quarterly schedule. Ordinary rent payments cover one month, and prepaid-rent usage is recognized monthly. Demand/capacity use monthly values, campaigns run one month, supplier purchases occur in October and are paid November 15, customer advances arrive in November and are earned December 20, and unpaid wages accrue December 31. Annual statements cover the short first accounting year September 1–December 31, with depreciation based on actual months in service. Prior-schedule saves can be exported and backed up before starting this schedule. The quarterly-named internal helper file remains for implementation compatibility but no quarterly workflow is active.
